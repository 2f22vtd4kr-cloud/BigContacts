1. Understand the current stage before doing anything
Apex Atlas is a model-led ReAct research bureau, not a deterministic enrichment pipeline.
* Groq Boss directs the case, evaluates progress and decides whether to continue, pivot, investigate a target or stop.
* Groq Right-hand independently evaluates evidence, contradictions and research gaps and advises Boss. It does not take over the Investigator’s research strategy.
* The selected Groq Investigator owns hypotheses, search queries, tool/capability choice, source selection, corroboration, falsification, pivots and stopping decisions.
* The deterministic runtime enforces authorization, network safety, capability availability, action contracts, evidence provenance, identity and admission rules, durable persistence, cancellation, budgets and truthful terminal outcomes.
Do not replace this architecture with a scripted pipeline. Do not introduce a mandatory sequence such as identity → company → LinkedIn → email. Do not force a target, source, query, provider, capability or card into existence.
The current evidence indicates that the model-led control loop executes live, but productive evidence-backed research and card creation have not yet been demonstrated end to end. The engineering goal is to diagnose and repair the actual failure boundaries.

4. What the audit must capture
Write chronologically, as each event occurs. Each entry should be specific enough that another engineer can reconstruct the actual runtime behavior without guessing.
A. Run and environment identity
Record the UTC timestamp, run number, job/case IDs, actual source SHA loaded by the running API, model/provider role, event or trajectory ID, iteration, terminal status and relevant runtime configuration. Record health and startup evidence only where relevant.
Do not assume that the workspace’s current Git HEAD is the same revision already loaded in the running process. Verify it.
B. Model communications and decisions
For Boss, Right-hand and every selected Investigator, capture:
* The actual model-visible request messages and prompts, after required redaction.
* The role, model identifier, request ID, attempt number and timestamp.
* The response format, structured-output schema and available tool/capability definitions.
* The actual visible structured response and model-selected action.
* The requested query, resource, provider, registry or other action arguments.
* Any explicit rationale or decision summary that the model itself returned as part of its ordinary output.
* The usage, finish reason, provider status and safe error diagnostics when available.
* Boss dispositions, Right-hand recommendations and the subsequent model-selected response or pivot.
Do not invent a rationale that is absent from the model’s response. Distinguish the model’s explicit decision summary from your own audit analysis.
Do not attempt to expose hidden chain-of-thought or private thought/reasoning fields. Record inspectable inputs, visible outputs, structured decisions and concise decision summaries instead.
C. Every Investigator action and runtime decision
For each proposed action, document the complete chain:
1. What the model proposed.
2. Which schema and runtime checks were applied.
3. Whether the action was accepted or blocked.
4. Which exact validation or authorization condition determined that result.
5. Whether an outbound request actually occurred.
6. The provider, destination and request outcome, when a request was sent.
7. The resulting observation, error, redirect, result count or fetched-page status.
8. What was saved durably.
9. What feedback was passed into the next model-visible prompt.
10. What happened on the next turn.
A blocked action is not an outbound request. Record it as blocked and explicitly state that no network request was made.
A search result or URL is a lead, not fetched evidence. A fetched page is not automatically a finding. A finding is not automatically an admitted candidate.
D. Errors and recoveries
Capture the exact safe diagnostic information needed to reproduce and classify each failure:
* HTTP status and provider error code/type.
* Sanitized error message and request ID.
* Model role and identifier.
* Strict-schema versus JSON-object response format.
* Relevant request parameters and completion budget.
* Retry number and whether the retry materially changed the request.
* Whether the failure occurred before generation, during structured-output generation/validation, during local action parsing, at authorization, in a tool/provider call, during evidence processing or during persistence.
* The recovery decision, result and remaining retry budget.
Distinguish provider-reported errors from runtime-generated validation failures. Do not label a 400 as authentication or quota exhaustion without evidence.
E. Research observations and evidence
Capture raw public search queries, provider names, result counts, public source URLs, fetch status, relevant source metadata, evidence identifiers, source provenance and safe excerpts that explain what was actually observed.
Track which results are merely leads versus successfully fetched pages and source-bound findings. Preserve contradictions, negative findings, source dependence, identity uncertainty and failed hypotheses.
Record contact attribution and its supporting source references, but do not place unredacted personal email addresses, phone numbers, access tokens, credentials or other sensitive contact values in Git-tracked audit text. Use redacted values and preserve the source URL, field type, attribution status and evidence identifier so the decision can be investigated without exposing the data itself.

C. Research productivity
Study the actual prompts, observations, trajectory, capability availability, evidence context and prior rejected actions. Determine whether the Investigator is:
* failing to capitalize on successful searches or fetched sources;
* repeating low-yield queries;
* ignoring useful observations;
* failing to form or falsify useful hypotheses;
* receiving insufficiently precise guard feedback;
* proposing unsupported actions despite available capability guidance;
* or reaching a legitimate stopping condition because evidence is insufficient.
Separate demonstrated model errors from defects in prompts, output contracts, action parsing, retrieval adapters, evidence binding or runtime feedback.
Do not prescribe a fixed query/tool sequence. Do not hard-code target selection, add a forced enrichment itinerary, or lower candidate-admission standards to obtain cards.

9. Definition of an honest end-to-end success
A run counts as successfully completing the intended bureau workflow only when the evidence demonstrates all relevant stages:
* The job launches and has a reconciled terminal outcome.
* Boss and Right-hand perform their intended roles.
* The selected Investigator makes model-owned, evidence-responsive decisions.
* Accepted searches and fetches actually execute.
* Fetched source observations and findings retain usable provenance.
* At least one genuinely qualifying person crosses the existing candidate-admission rules, if the research evidence supports one.
* The admitted candidate and its evidence are durably persisted.
* The normal bureau UI displays the card from that durable state.
* Final counts and audit events agree with the result.
* The audit explains all significant errors, blocks, recoveries and remaining limitations.
If the research cannot substantiate a candidate, no card should be manufactured to satisfy the goal. Report an honest incomplete or no-qualified-candidate outcome and identify the precise bottleneck.
