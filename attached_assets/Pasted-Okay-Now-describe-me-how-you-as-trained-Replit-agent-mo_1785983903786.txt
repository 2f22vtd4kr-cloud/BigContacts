Okay. Now describe me how you as trained Replit agent model would search for the next batch of HNWI's contacts?
I would not begin by searching for email addresses. I would begin by building a defensible batch of real people or entities, then investigate their public routes in stages.
1. Define the batch
First I would set a strict batch contract:
* Jurisdictions and registries to use
* Target entity types: individuals, companies, trusts, property vehicles, aircraft owners, etc.
* Maximum number of admitted targets
* Public-source requirement
* Whether the goal is discovery, ownership research, contactability, or all three
The admission cap would apply to the whole run, not separately to every source. That prevents one provider from flooding the batch with low-quality candidates.
2. Discover candidates from authoritative sources
I would start with real public records, such as:
* Aircraft and aviation ownership records
* SEC/EDGAR filings
* Companies House or other corporate registries
* Property and land-registry data
* Public foundation and nonprofit filings
* Confirmed investigative datasets
* Public ownership and corporate relationship records
At this stage, a record is only a candidate. It is not yet accepted as an HNWI.
I would reject or quarantine:
* Placeholder names
* Role-shaped names such as “Deputy” or “Family Office”
* Editorial or recipe text accidentally extracted as names
* Famous people used only because they are famous
* Same-name matches without identity evidence
* Businesses incorrectly treated as people
3. Build an identity bundle
For every admitted candidate, I would collect:
* Legal and trading names
* Registry identifiers
* Country and jurisdiction
* Known companies
* Public biographies or roles
* Relevant aliases
* Source URLs
* Evidence explaining why this record refers to this person or entity
The system should not rely on name matching alone. A name becomes useful only when combined with identifiers, geography, corporate relationships, filings, or other corroborating evidence.
4. Separate wealth from identity and access
I would score these independently:
1. Identity — Is this the correct person or entity?
2. Wealth or asset evidence — What public evidence supports financial significance?
3. Ownership/control — Does the person own, control, direct, or merely operate something?
4. Contactability — Is there a public route?
5. Access/reachability — Is there a realistic path to the person?
For example, owning an aircraft may support an asset signal, but it does not prove that a person owns a company. Being listed as a director does not automatically prove beneficial ownership. A public company email does not prove personal access.
5. Run anchored web research
Only after the candidate has a useful identity bundle would I send bounded research queries.
Each query would use safe anchors such as:
* Legal name
* Trading name
* City and country
* Registry identifiers
* Known company names
* Candidate official domains
I would not forward raw private residence text as a search anchor.
The research lanes would be distinct:
* Official records and filings
* People, biographies, and press
* Company ownership and parent groups
* Official contact routes
* Operator and management-company pages
* Public asset and registry evidence
Provider output would be treated as leads. It would not be accepted as truth merely because an AI model or search provider returned it.
6. Follow the relationship chain
For a company or property vehicle, I would investigate in this order:
1. Identify the exact business or legal entity.
2. Find directors, officers, operators, owners, and parent companies.
3. Follow the operator or management company.
4. Inspect official team, contact, board, and investment pages.
5. Identify named executives or principals.
6. Search for increasingly direct public routes.
7. Preserve the indirect routes even when a more direct route is found.
For example:
Property vehicle
→ registered company
→ operator or parent company
→ named executive
→ official executive phone/email
→ manual review route

The C/O company, operator, and parent company are research leads. They are not automatically proof of ownership.
7. Validate every contact candidate
For each email, phone number, or social profile, I would ask:
* Was the value observed on an exact public page?
* Does that page clearly identify the person?
* Is the value located within the person’s page section rather than somewhere else on the page?
* Does the domain belong to the relevant organization?
* Is there independent corroboration?
* Is there a conflict with another candidate?
* Is this a generic shared mailbox?
* Is this actually a registry or office contact?
Important rules:
* Never construct an email address from a guessed pattern.
* Never promote a generic info@, contact@, or office mailbox to a person.
* Never treat a social username as proof of identity.
* Never treat a phone number near a company name as a personal phone.
* Never use a provider label as corroboration; corroboration requires independent source domains or claim-level evidence.
* Preserve rejected candidates and the reason for rejection.
8. Rank routes instead of flattening them
The output should be a route hierarchy, for example:
1. Validated direct personal route
2. Named executive or principal route
3. Operator or parent-company route
4. Intermediary or management-company route
5. Organization route
6. Registry or filing route
A named executive route can be extremely useful without being promoted as the target’s personal contact. It remains a manual-review route with its source page and relationship explanation.
9. Use conservative promotion rules
Only evidence that passes identity, attribution, and source checks could populate a direct contact field.
Possible outcomes would be:
* verified_direct_route
* attribution_review
* organization_contact
* evidence_only
* rejected
* no_usable_public_route
For many HNWI candidates, the correct result will be “evidence found, no verified personal contact.” That is better than creating a misleading contact.
10. Run enrichment in bounded batches
I would use:
* Small discovery batches
* One target at a time for expensive enrichment
* Per-target timeouts
* Provider-specific rate limits
* Retry queues only for failed or incomplete targets
* No automatic reprocessing of the entire batch after one provider failure
* Separate progress for discovery, enrichment, and research review
The batch dashboard should show:
* Candidates discovered
* Candidates admitted
* Candidates rejected
* Identity-confirmed targets
* Wealth/ownership evidence
* Public contact candidates
* Verified direct routes
* Organization-only routes
* Manual-review routes
* Provider failures and source gaps
What I would explicitly avoid
I would not:
* Scrape private accounts or bypass authentication
* Use leaked, purchased, or non-public personal data
* Guess emails or phone numbers
* Treat wealth estimates as identity proof
* Turn every company officer into an HNWI
* Treat fame, social presence, or an expensive asset as contactability
* Generate automatic outreach
* Promote organization routes as personal access
* Let an LLM silently admit candidates or contacts
The core principle is:
Discover broadly, verify narrowly, preserve indirect routes, and promote almost nothing unless the evidence clearly supports the exact person and the exact contact route.

Recommended provider orchestration
For each target, I would use separate lanes:
Lane A — Identity and official records
Lane B — Ownership and control
Lane C — People, executives, and operators
Lane D — Official contact routes
Lane E — Independent corroboration
Lane F — Contradiction and negative search

Each provider should receive the same compact target fingerprint but a different query lane. Then the application should normalize all results into:
candidate → source-linked → attribution review → independently corroborated → verified route

The final system should never treat “the provider returned an email” as equivalent to “the person has a verified public email.”
